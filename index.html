The challenges

The original series, built around the functions that do most of the heavy lifting in real modelling work. Six functions, each with its own workbook and eight levels that ramp up as you go.

XLOOKUP The one that quietly retires VLOOKUP, HLOOKUP and a lot of your old INDEX/MATCH habits. Learn it well and half your lookup headaches disappear.
XMATCH XLOOKUP's quieter sibling, and the tool for positional logic. Once it clicks, you start reaching for it in places you'd never have thought to before.
SORT & SORTBY Sort data with a formula instead of a button, so it re-sorts itself as the numbers change. Live reports stop needing a manual refresh.
FILTER The function that makes most of your old helper columns redundant. Pull exactly the rows you want, and watch them update on their own.
UNIQUE Deceptively simple on its own, surprisingly powerful the moment you pair it with FILTER and SORT. The backbone of dynamic lists and dashboards.
INDEX The old classic that still earns its place every day. Nothing else gives you quite the same control over pulling values out of a range.

A six-part series on writing your own reusable logic in Excel. This is where you stop copying the same nested formula everywhere and start building tools you can name and reuse.

LET Name the pieces of a big formula so it reads like plain steps instead of one unreadable line. Easier to follow, and it recalculates faster too.
LAMBDA Write your own custom functions, in Excel, with no code. Solve a fiddly problem once and reuse it everywhere by name.
SCAN · REDUCE · MAP · BYROW · BYCOL The helper functions that let LAMBDA loop over your data. This is how you handle whole ranges at once without dragging a formula down a thousand rows.

Modern Excel's least understood behaviours: the moments when a calculation in one cell doesn't give you one cell of output. Get these right and your formulas start doing far more with far less.

Spilling Why a single formula can fill a whole range of cells on its own, and how to work with that instead of fighting it. The foundation everything else builds on.
Lifting How functions that expect one value quietly run across a whole array instead. Understand it and you'll write one formula where you used to write hundreds.
Broadcasting How Excel pairs up a row and a column to build a full grid of results. The trick behind clean calculation tables with no repeated formulas.
